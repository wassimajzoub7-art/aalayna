01 · Your brief

Block

Square capitals on a 5 × 5 grid of modules: the same squares a QR code is made of, and the QR is how every guest meets you. The weight is heavy enough to hold at 16 pixels and on a thermal receipt.

You asked for two rows of three. 3layna fits that exactly, and its 3 is the ع itself: it is how every Lebanese phone already writes the word, so it gets the red. Spelled Aalayna, the grid needs a fourth column and the red moves to a full stop in the spare cell.

Grid
Every letter is 5 × 5 modules; one module is the stroke. Letters and lines are one module apart.
Corners
Outer corners turn on 1.5 modules, inner on 0.5, so each bend is one even stroke. Stroke ends are cut square.
Optics
Horizontals are 8% lighter than verticals. Drawn equal, they would look heavier.
The N
The only line off the grid. An arched N reads as a lowercase n; the diagonal keeps the name legible.
The 3
Its middle bar starts two modules in, so it reads as a 3 and not a reversed E. It is the only red letter.
Red
Marks what is Arabic: the 3 here, the dots in the Kufi. Spelled AALAYNA there is no 3, so the red becomes a full stop.

02 · My read

Kufi

The name is an Arabic phrase before it is a brand: علينا, “it’s on us”, the line every table in Lebanon argues over when the bill comes. Square Kufic has built Arabic out of square modules for centuries, in brick and tile, long before QR codes. So the name gets drawn in its own script, and the grid you wanted is already in it.

Reads
Right to left: ع ل ي ن ا. A hook, a tall stroke, two short teeth, a tall stroke, on one baseline.
Grid
12 × 12 modules, with the Block’s module and corners. The tall strokes are twice the height of the teeth.
The dots
ي and ن are the same tooth; only their dots tell them apart. The three dots are the only red.
Square
It is its own icon: the app, the WhatsApp picture, the middle of a QR code. The Block needs a separate one.

The system

One module, two scripts

Same stroke, same corners, same baseline. The Latin capitals stand exactly as tall as the Kufi teeth, so either logo can sit next to the other without adjustment.

Red marks the Arabic

The 3 and the three dots, never another letter. On a red ground they turn ink.

Keep the grid

Don’t stretch, outline or respace. Clear space is two modules on every side.

Smallest sizes

Block: 16 px as the icon, 64 px wide on one line. Kufi: 24 px as the icon; under 32 px, set the Latin name beside it.

My pick

3LAYNA, if you can own the name.

With 3layna the Block becomes the stronger logo: it is your brief with nothing forced, and the red 3 tells a Lebanese guest the brand is theirs before they have read it. The Kufi becomes its Arabic signature, for Arabic menus, the door sticker, and anywhere the name reads right to left.

The catch: 3layna.com is already registered, and it isn’t yours. People type what they see. If you can’t get it, stay with Aalayna: the switch at the top shows the same system still works.
